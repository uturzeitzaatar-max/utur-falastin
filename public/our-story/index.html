BEIT UMMAR, PALESTINE

The woman behind "Utur"

In Beit Ummar, a Palestinian town in the West Bank, there lived a beautiful woman. The people of the town called her عطر (UTUR), which means fragrance in Arabic. They gave her that nickname because of her beauty.

She was my grandmother

My grandmother and grandfather built a life together in Beit Ummar. When he died of cancer, she raised their family on her own and kept the businesses they had built going. She ran a workshop that made stone blocks for people’s homes. On her land, she grew olives, grapes, and other fruits, and sold what she harvested. I like to think of how much of her work became part of other people’s lives, from the stone in their walls to the olives on their tables.

That same land was threatened with confiscation under the Israeli occupation. Her business was threatened with demolition. My grandmother protested. A photograph of her at one of those protests was widely shared. When I look at it now, I think about the years of work behind that moment. She had raised a family there, earned a living there, and tended the same land season after season. She knew what she stood to lose. She kept resisting until the end of her life.

Carrying on her work

She died in 2015. Ten years later, I wanted people to know who she was, and I wanted to carry on the work she had begun. I started UTUR with the things I already associated with her and with home, especially olives and grapes.

When I put عطر on a bottle of olive oil, I think of Beit Ummar and the people who once called my grandmother by that name. I hope someone who sees it on their own table will come to know a little of her story, too.
